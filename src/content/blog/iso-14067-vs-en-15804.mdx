## PCF and EPD are not the same deliverable

Both involve LCA. Both report per product. Both lean on ISO. They are still different artifacts, and the standard tells you which one you are building.

A **product carbon footprint** is a single impact category: GWP, normally GWP100. **ISO 14067** specifies the requirements. One number, one boundary, one reviewer question — "is the GWP characterization defensible?"

An **EPD** is multi-impact and lives inside the **ISO 14025 / ISO 21930** declaration framework. It needs a Product Category Rule, third-party verification, and — for construction products — a core PCR that governs which life-cycle modules you report.

That core PCR is **EN 15804**.

---

## EN 15804 governs the modules, not just the math

The reason EN 15804 matters is mechanical, not philosophical. It defines the module structure an EPD declares: A1–A3 (product stage — raw material supply, transport, manufacturing), A4–A5, the B-modules, C1–C4, and module D. An EPD that reports A1–A3 only is a different document from one that runs cradle-to-grave — and EN 15804 is the rule that says which modules are mandatory for your product category and how each is bounded.

ISO 14067 does not give you that module table. It governs the GWP indicator — how the carbon number itself is characterized. So a construction-product EPD typically does both: EN 15804 sets the boundary and the modules; ISO 14067 informs the GWP100 figure inside them.

> A standalone product carbon footprint answers to ISO 14067. A construction-product EPD answers to EN 15804 — with ISO 14067 informing the GWP indicator it carries.

That is the whole decision, and it is not a generic summary. It is a boundary implication: choose EN 15804 and you owe a module breakdown; choose ISO 14067 and you owe a single defensible GWP100 figure with its system boundary stated.

---

## The wrong call costs system boundary

Pick the standard your reviewer didn't expect and the damage shows up in three places.

The **boundary** shifts: a 14067 PCF stated cradle-to-gate does not satisfy an EN 15804 EPD that mandates C-modules for end-of-life. The **modules** change: an EPD reviewer reading for A1–A3 plus D will reject a flat per-kg number with no module split. And the **GWP indicator** has to reconcile: the characterization that passed your PCF still has to sit correctly inside the EPD's declared unit.

None of this is exotic. It is the routine cost of answering the standard question late instead of first.
