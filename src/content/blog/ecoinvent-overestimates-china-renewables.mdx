<span class="dropcap">P</span>ull the GWP100 factor for one kWh of Chinese solar PV from Ecoinvent v3.12.0, cut-off — the system model that carries GWP100 — and you get 0.080 kg CO2-eq. Pull the same kWh from HiQLCD v1.4.0, the China LCI database, and you get 0.029. The Ecoinvent number is 176% higher. That gap is now in your export carbon label, your CBAM filing, your carbon-market price. It is not a rounding question. It decides whether your product reads as clean or dirty at the border.

Same kWh. Same horizon. Same characterization. The number you ship depends entirely on which database your background data came from. Here is why the two disagree, where the disagreement is real and where it is an artifact, and what your tooling should do when it sees a spread that wide.

## The spread, by source

Five China electricity sources, GWP100, kg CO2-eq per kWh, HiQLCD v1.4.0 against Ecoinvent v3.12.0 cut-off:

- **Solar PV** — 0.029 vs 0.080. Ecoinvent +176%.
- **Wind, onshore** — 0.009 vs 0.031. Ecoinvent +244%.
- **Coal thermal** — 1.033 vs 1.210. Ecoinvent +17%.
- **Run-of-river hydro** — 0.012 vs 0.005. HiQLCD higher.
- **Nuclear PWR** — 0.006 vs 0.008. Close.

Read the pattern before you read the cause. The two databases agree closely on nuclear. They sit within a coal plant's noise on thermal. They invert on hydro. They diverge by more than 2x on exactly the two sources your low-carbon story leans on: solar and wind.

## Where the gap comes from

Ecoinvent has no China-built scenario data for manufacturing the panels and the turbines. For those processes it transplants European and global manufacturing inventories. So the Chinese PV cell carries the energy intensity of a cell line that was never in China, and the onshore turbine carries a supply chain that was never built here. China manufactures most of the world's PV cells and a large share of its turbines, at an efficiency the transplanted inventory does not see. The result is a manufacturing burden inflated for the two technologies where China's industrial scale matters most.

The electricity mix underneath compounds it. Ecoinvent models China electricity as 33 provincial hard-coal datasets plus global generics. HiQLCD models it from 4,862 China energy datasets — 4,256 energy-type records, resolved across 31 provinces at provincial precision. When the grid that powers the factory is itself a coal-weighted generic, the manufacturing footprint inherits that generic's carbon, and the error stacks on the error already in the transplanted inventory.

> The Ecoinvent solar number is not measuring China's solar. It is measuring Europe's solar, billed to China.

Not every gap is an Ecoinvent artifact. On run-of-river hydro, HiQLCD reads *higher* — 0.012 against 0.005 — because HiQLCD allocates reservoir-construction burden into the per-kWh figure and Ecoinvent's boundary does not. That is a system-boundary disagreement, not an accuracy defect: a question about what counts as part of generating the electricity. Nuclear (0.006 vs 0.008) and coal (1.033 vs 1.210) sit close; the residual difference traces to the uranium-mining and fuel-cycle boundary on one, and to a mine-CHP extreme value Ecoinvent carries on the other. Knowing which kind of gap you are looking at is the whole job. One you correct. One you document.

## The averaging trap

Most automated factor-matching does the convenient thing with a 0.029-versus-0.080 spread: it picks one, or it splits the difference. Either move buries the most important fact about your solar row — that two credible databases disagree about it by 176% — under a single tidy number your auditor cannot reconstruct. Average the two and you get 0.054, a figure no database holds and no one can defend. Pick silently and you have made a methodology decision your reasoning chain never records.

This is the failure mode the Audit Trail exists to name. The spread *is* the signal. Hiding it is the error.
